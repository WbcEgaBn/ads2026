WINE DATA ANALYSIS AND NEURAL NETWORK REPORT

1. Data inspection
I examined Combined_Wine.csv, which contains 6,497 wines and 14 variables:
11 chemical measurements, quality score, wine type, and quality class.
There were no missing values and 1,177 exact duplicate rows. White wines
accounted for 4,898 rows and red wines for 1,599 rows.

2. Exploratory analysis and visualizations
I created wine_visuals.py to generate a distribution chart for every
variable, boxplots, and correlation matrices. Alcohol had the strongest
positive linear association with quality (Pearson r = 0.44). Density
(r = -0.31) and volatile acidity (r = -0.27) were negatively associated
with quality. These associations do not establish cause and effect.

I then compared relationships within each wine type. An interesting
finding was that volatile acidity and total sulfur dioxide correlated
negatively across all wines (r = -0.41), but slightly positively within
red wines (r = 0.08) and white wines (r = 0.09). Differences between the
wine types help explain this reversal. Residual sugar and density were
also more strongly correlated in white wines (r = 0.84) than red wines
(r = 0.35). Additional boxplots and grouped charts illustrate these patterns.

3. Neural network preparation and training
I created train_wine_neural_network.py to predict the numerical quality
score from all 11 chemical measurements and encoded wine type.
quality_class was excluded because it is derived from the quality score
and would reveal information about the answer.

After removing exact duplicates, I split the 5,320 remaining rows into
3,724 training, 1,064 validation, and 532 test rows (70/20/10).
Identical inputs stayed in the same split, and scaling used training
data only to prevent information leakage.

I compared two PyTorch networks with hidden layers of (32, 16) and (64, 32)
neurons. Validation error selected the (64, 32) network at epoch 81, using
early stopping. The test set was evaluated after model selection.

4. Results and evaluation visuals
Split          Exact accuracy   Within one point   Mean absolute error
Training          59.7%              97.6%              0.493
Validation        54.9%              96.0%              0.542
Test              58.5%              96.8%              0.516

Accuracy uses rounded predictions; error uses continuous predictions
and is measured in quality points. Test exact accuracy exceeded a mean
baseline (43.4%) and ridge regression (56.0%), with only a modest gain
over ridge regression.

Evaluation visuals include learning curves, split accuracy, a confusion
matrix, prediction errors, and baseline comparisons. Model weights,
predictions, and metrics are saved in wine_neural_network. I verified
split separation and reproduced the results from the saved model.

5. Limitations
Quality scores of 5 and 6 dominate the dataset. The model performed worse
on rare extreme scores, so the high within-one-point accuracy should be
read alongside exact accuracy and error by score. Results come from one
fixed split and may differ with other splits or new wine populations.

To regenerate the outputs:
python3 wine_visuals.py
python3 train_wine_neural_network.py
